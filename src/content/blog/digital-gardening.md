Chronological streams treat ideas like perishable fruit: fresh today, rotten tomorrow. The social web conditioned us to think that if an essay was written six months ago, it is historical trivia, irrelevant to the present discourse.

Digital gardening is an intentional rejection of the timeline.

## Plants, Not Broadcasts

In a digital garden, pages are not published once and frozen into stone. They are planted as seeds, watered through revisions, pruned when superseded, and allowed to cross-pollinate with adjacent thoughts.

- **Seeds**: Half-formed fragments, questions, and reading highlights.
- **Saplings**: Structured outlines with emerging arguments.
- **Evergreens**: Mature essays that have stood the test of repeated scrutiny.

When you abandon the pressure of the broadcast, writing becomes an act of patient cultivation rather than performance art.
